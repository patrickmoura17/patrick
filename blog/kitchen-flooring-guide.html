Vinyl, hardwood or tile: the best flooring for a kitchen

Flooring · 4 min read

A kitchen floor deals with spills, dropped pans, chair legs and hours of standing. Here's how the four most popular options hold up.

Luxury vinyl plank (LVP)

Rigid-core vinyl plank is waterproof, comfortable underfoot and affordable, and the best products look convincingly like wood. Durability depends on the wear layer, measured in mils. For a kitchen, look for 12 mil or more, and 20 mil if you have big dogs or a busy household. Vinyl can dent under heavy appliances and can't be refinished, but individual planks can be replaced.

Hardwood

Solid hardwood is warm, adds value and can be sanded and refinished several times. Water is its weakness: wipe spills quickly and use mats at the sink and dishwasher. Solid boards also expand and shrink with humidity, which matters in a humid climate.

Engineered wood

Engineered wood has a real hardwood top layer over a cross-layered core, which makes it much more stable when humidity changes. You get the look and feel of real wood with fewer gaps and less cupping. A thick top layer can be refinished once or twice.

Porcelain tile

Porcelain is the most durable choice. It's waterproof, scratch-resistant and comes in wood-look and stone-look styles. The downsides: it's hard and cold underfoot (a good mat or radiant heat helps), and dropped glasses won't survive. Choose a matte finish for better grip.

Before or after the cabinets?

This question causes a lot of remodel delays. Tile and nail-down hardwood usually go in before the cabinets, so the floor runs underneath and you can change the layout later. Floating floors, such as most vinyl plank and laminate, usually go in after the cabinets. Heavy cabinets on top of a floating floor stop it from expanding and can cause buckling. Either way, plan the floor height so the dishwasher can still slide in and out.

Quick picks

  • Kids, pets, spills, on a budget: luxury vinyl plank.
  • Real wood with fewer worries: engineered wood.
  • Maximum durability: porcelain tile.
  • Matching wood floors in the rest of the house: hardwood, with good mats.
